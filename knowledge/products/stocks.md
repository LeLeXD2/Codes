# Stocks

## Overview

A stock, also known as a share or equity, represents partial ownership in a company. When an investor purchases a stock, they become a shareholder and own a small portion of that business.

Companies issue stocks to raise money for expansion, research, hiring employees, or developing new products. In return, investors gain the opportunity to benefit from the company's future growth through increases in share price and, in some cases, dividend payments.

Stocks are one of the most popular investment vehicles because they have historically provided higher long-term returns than many other asset classes, although they also involve greater risk.

---

# How Do Stocks Work?

When a company becomes publicly listed on a stock exchange, investors can buy and sell its shares throughout the trading day.

The value of a stock changes continuously as investors react to:

* Company performance
* Financial results
* Economic conditions
* Industry developments
* Investor sentiment
* Supply and demand

If demand for a stock exceeds supply, the share price generally rises.

If more investors want to sell than buy, the share price generally falls.

---

# Why Do Companies Issue Stocks?

Companies sell shares to raise capital.

The money raised can be used for:

* Expanding operations
* Developing new products
* Investing in research and development
* Paying existing debt
* Acquiring other businesses
* Hiring additional employees

Unlike loans, companies generally do not need to repay investors directly for the capital raised through issuing shares.

---

# Why Do Investors Buy Stocks?

Investors purchase stocks for several reasons.

## Capital Appreciation

If a company's value increases over time, its share price may also increase.

Investors can potentially earn a profit by selling the stock at a higher price than they originally paid.

---

## Dividend Income

Some companies distribute part of their profits to shareholders through dividends.

Not all companies pay dividends.

Many growth companies choose to reinvest profits back into expanding their business instead.

---

## Long-Term Wealth Creation

Many investors buy stocks as part of a long-term investment strategy aimed at building wealth over many years.

---

# Common Types of Stocks

## Growth Stocks

Growth companies focus on expanding rapidly.

Characteristics include:

* High expected revenue growth.
* Limited or no dividends.
* Higher price volatility.
* Greater long-term growth potential.

Technology companies often fall into this category.

---

## Value Stocks

Value stocks are companies that appear to trade below their estimated intrinsic value.

They may offer:

* Lower valuation ratios.
* More stable businesses.
* Moderate growth potential.

---

## Dividend Stocks

Dividend stocks regularly distribute part of their profits to shareholders.

Many investors use dividend stocks to generate passive income.

---

# Benefits of Investing in Stocks

Stocks offer several advantages.

* Potential for long-term capital growth.
* Opportunity to receive dividend income.
* Ownership in successful businesses.
* High liquidity through stock exchanges.
* Ability to participate in economic growth.

---

# Risks of Investing in Stocks

Stocks also involve risks.

Examples include:

* Share prices may decline.
* Companies may perform poorly.
* Economic recessions.
* Market volatility.
* Company bankruptcy.

Individual stocks generally carry more risk than diversified investment products such as ETFs or index funds.

---

# Practical Example

Suppose an investor purchases shares of Apple.

If Apple introduces successful new products and generates higher profits, investor confidence may increase, causing the share price to rise.

However, if Apple reports disappointing earnings or experiences declining sales, the share price may fall.

This illustrates that stock prices reflect investors' expectations about a company's future performance.

---

# Common Misconceptions

### Buying a stock means owning the entire company.

False.

Purchasing a stock means owning only a small fraction of the company.

---

### Expensive stocks are always better investments.

False.

A higher share price does not necessarily indicate that a company is a better investment.

Investors should evaluate the company's financial performance, competitive position, and future growth prospects.

---

### Stocks always increase in value.

False.

Stock prices can both rise and fall, sometimes significantly.

---

### You need a large amount of money to buy stocks.

False.

Many brokers now support fractional investing, allowing investors to purchase portions of a share.

---

# Best Practices

* Research companies before investing.
* Diversify across multiple stocks.
* Invest according to your financial goals.
* Avoid emotional buying and selling.
* Maintain a long-term perspective.
* Continue learning about financial markets.

# Frequently Asked Questions

### What is a stock?

A stock represents partial ownership in a publicly listed company.

---

### How do investors make money from stocks?

Investors may earn money through:

* Capital appreciation.
* Dividend payments.

Neither is guaranteed.

---

### Are stocks risky?

Yes.

Stock prices fluctuate because of company performance, market conditions, and investor sentiment.

---

### Should beginners invest in individual stocks?

Beginners may invest in individual stocks, but many start with diversified ETFs or index funds to reduce risk.

---

### Do all stocks pay dividends?

No.

Many companies, particularly growth companies, choose to reinvest profits rather than distribute dividends.
