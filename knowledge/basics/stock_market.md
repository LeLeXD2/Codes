# Stock Market

## Overview

The stock market is a marketplace where investors buy and sell shares of publicly listed companies. When investors purchase a stock, they become partial owners of that company and may benefit if the company's value increases or if it distributes dividends.

The stock market plays an important role in the global economy by allowing companies to raise capital for growth while giving investors opportunities to build wealth over time.

Although stock prices fluctuate every day, the stock market has historically generated positive long-term returns despite periods of volatility and economic uncertainty.

---

# How Does the Stock Market Work?

Companies that wish to raise money can issue shares through a stock exchange. Investors purchase these shares, and the price changes continuously based on supply and demand.

If many investors want to buy a stock, its price generally rises.

If many investors want to sell a stock, its price generally falls.

Stock prices constantly adjust as new information becomes available.

---

# Stock Exchanges

A stock exchange is an organised marketplace where stocks are traded.

Some of the world's largest stock exchanges include:

* New York Stock Exchange (NYSE)
* Nasdaq
* London Stock Exchange (LSE)
* Singapore Exchange (SGX)
* Tokyo Stock Exchange (TSE)

Each exchange has listing requirements that companies must meet before their shares can be traded publicly.

---

# Why Do Stock Prices Change?

Stock prices move because investors constantly reassess what a company is worth.

Factors that influence stock prices include:

## Company Performance

Strong earnings, revenue growth, or successful new products often increase investor confidence.

Poor financial results may cause prices to fall.

---

## Economic Conditions

Interest rates, inflation, unemployment, and economic growth influence investor expectations.

Changes in these factors can affect the entire stock market.

---

## Market Sentiment

Investor emotions such as optimism, fear, or uncertainty can move prices even when company fundamentals remain unchanged.

---

## News and Events

Examples include:

* Earnings announcements
* New product launches
* Government policies
* Geopolitical events
* Natural disasters
* Technological breakthroughs

---

# Bull Markets and Bear Markets

## Bull Market

A bull market refers to a period where stock prices generally rise over an extended period.

Characteristics include:

* Strong investor confidence.
* Economic growth.
* Rising company profits.
* Increasing stock prices.

---

## Bear Market

A bear market occurs when stock prices decline significantly, typically by 20% or more from recent highs.

Characteristics include:

* Lower investor confidence.
* Economic uncertainty.
* Increased market volatility.
* Falling stock prices.

Bear markets are a normal part of long-term investing and have historically been followed by periods of recovery.

---

# Benefits of the Stock Market

The stock market provides several advantages:

* Opportunity to build long-term wealth.
* Ownership in successful companies.
* Potential dividend income.
* Liquidity, allowing investors to buy or sell shares during trading hours.
* Access to diversified investments through ETFs and index funds.

---

# Risks of the Stock Market

Investing in the stock market also involves risks.

Examples include:

* Market volatility.
* Economic recessions.
* Company bankruptcy.
* Unexpected global events.
* Changes in investor sentiment.

Short-term losses are possible, even for high-quality companies.

---

# Practical Example

Suppose Company ABC reports stronger-than-expected quarterly earnings.

Many investors become optimistic and begin purchasing the stock.

As demand increases, the share price rises.

Conversely, if the company later reports declining profits, investors may sell their shares, causing the stock price to decrease.

This illustrates how expectations and new information influence stock prices.

# Common Misconceptions

### The stock market is gambling.

False.

While prices fluctuate daily, investing is based on analysing businesses, financial performance, and economic conditions rather than pure chance.

---

### The stock market always goes up.

False.

Markets experience corrections, bear markets, and periods of volatility.

However, many major stock markets have historically shown long-term growth despite temporary declines.

---

### A falling stock price always means a company is failing.

False.

Stock prices can decline because of temporary market sentiment, broader economic conditions, or short-term uncertainty even when a company's fundamentals remain strong.

---

### You need to monitor the market every day.

False.

Long-term investors often review their portfolios periodically rather than reacting to daily price movements.

---

# Best Practices

* Invest with a long-term perspective.
* Diversify across multiple companies and sectors.
* Avoid making emotional decisions during market volatility.
* Continue learning about financial markets.
* Focus on company fundamentals rather than short-term price movements.
* Invest according to your financial goals and risk tolerance.

# Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the stock market?

The stock market is a marketplace where investors buy and sell ownership shares of publicly listed companies.

---

### Why do stock prices change every day?

Prices change because investors continuously buy and sell stocks based on company performance, economic conditions, and market sentiment.

---

### Can the stock market crash?

Yes.

Market crashes have occurred throughout history. While crashes can cause significant short-term losses, markets have historically recovered over the long term.

---

### Is the stock market suitable for beginners?

Yes.

Many beginners start by investing in diversified ETFs or index funds before investing in individual stocks.

---

### Should I panic when the market falls?

Generally, no.

Short-term market declines are a normal part of investing. Many long-term investors continue investing during market downturns instead of selling out of fear.
